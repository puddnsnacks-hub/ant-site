Summary

Senior UX designer specializing in design system authoring and management. After around 20 years in human-computer interaction (HCI), I have come to believe that just about everything is better when there are rules to preserve its identity.

I have managed design systems for Google Meet and Duo and spent a three-month contract getting YouTube's system neat and orderly. I created a modular marketing design system for the redesign of Gillette.com and built another for joy+glee from scratch. I have worked on three AAA video games spanning Xbox, PlayStation, and PC, creating menu architecture, uniting UX across platforms, and contributing heavily to defining the brand.

I now author design systems specifically for AI agents, keeping their builds predictable and consistent through measurable standards and rules enforced by gates.

  • AI-consumable design systems. Contracts, tokens, and accessibility requirements published as typed data an agent reads, not prose it interprets.
  • Governance as gates, not documents. Rules written as automated checks that fail a build.
  • Agent evaluation. Blind trials that measure whether an agent obeys the system, scored against a sequestered answer key.

Experience

Lead UX/UI Designer

Lead or sole UX designer on every engagement: federal, telecom, healthcare, and consumer. The contract scope was design and theming. Engineering owned component implementation and the USWDS base, and I owned the tokens, contracts, rules, and documentation they built against.

A federal department – Design System Lead October 2025 – September 2026

  • Rebuilt the federal design system as a contract an AI build agent reads, with three tiers of tokens, components, patterns, and page templates published as typed data instead of prose. The agent gets the component, the variant, and the properties to set. Nothing is left to infer.
  • Independently rebuilt the federal design system in about two weeks as an AI-optimized version that went further than the original, which took two quarters with an engineer coding my designs.
  • Designed the component library in Figma and established the shared rules that unite it.
  • Wrote the system's rules as automated checks that run at build time and on every finished page, so a broken rule fails the build instead of surviving review.
  • Strengthened guardrails with gates that stop a release when a rule is broken.
  • Developed sealed build-agent sessions as a gate for version publishing.
  • Designed the blind trial method that measures whether agents obey the system: seeded content packs, a sequestered answer key, and fresh agent sessions. In three head-to-head comparisons, the contract-first system won on both criteria.
  • Delivered the prototype to engineering, whose independent evaluation recommended adopting its tokens, contracts, rules, and documentation approach and retargeting its components.
  • Specified the conversational component family for AI chat and agent experiences, benchmarked against other design systems, with a visual rule to alert users that they are dealing with AI.
  • Wrote the rules for how an AI agent conducts itself in the interface: it takes turns, is proactive in service of being productive, shows its work, and asks before it acts.
  • Moved the system's source of truth from Figma to machine-readable contracts, keeping Figma as a sketchpad with a layer generated from the source code, so edits start from fact.

Universal Destinations and Experiences – Design Systems

  • Added a theme layer of color tokens, logo containers, imagery, and decorative rules driven by destination, park, land, attraction, event, or season, so one layout serves every property. Variants come from hiding optional modules and flexing repeatable groups, never from new templates.
  • Built the Universal Email Design System: eight base templates assembled from elements, layout modules, and content blocks, with a content category locked to each slot so every email of a type does the same job. It replaced a library of rigid one-offs the team called a “sea of blue.”
